* Problem

{Open with a concrete example — specific scenario / input-output / failure screenshot / user story — let the reader experience the dilemma in this example. Describe in one or two sentences.}

{Three-part narrative, threaded through the same example: (1) Experience the dilemma (2) Why old solutions couldn't work on this example — expose shortcomings on the same example (3) What this paper's authors saw that others missed — only hint at the direction, don't unfold the mechanism.}

{If no clear predecessor, skip (2), go directly "Experience → New Opening".}

* Translation

{Don't restate the problem. Continue on the concrete example from "Problem", unfolding the paper's method step by step on it.}

{Open with an anchor — the anchor is what the method looks like on that example (e.g. "on that Turing Award problem, the method is like a triage desk"). Each paragraph advances one step, giving the reader "ah, I see" mini-revelations. Revelation verbs: "At this point you see..." "So then..." "How do they solve this step — they thought of..."}

{Cover (all on that example): how it works, how well it works, key concepts for understanding the full text. Subheadings organized by content need.}

{Exemption: only when mechanism details can't be supported by the same example, introduce a "sub-example" as a magnifying glass — maintain the parent example's problem domain, don't change maps.}

* Core Concepts

{Pick 3 key concepts needed to understand this paper, deconstruct one by one — each returns to the "Problem" example to land.}

{Each concept: one sentence saying what it is, what it looks like on that example (what would happen without it on that example), why missing it means you can't follow what comes next. Each concept unlocked clears more fog, deepens understanding of the example another layer.}

* Insight

{One sentence: the intellectual crystal this paper truly found.}

{Stripped of the paper's context, does this sentence itself still have force? Don't restate the conclusion — distill what you yourself saw. If there's none, say so directly.}

* Advisor Review

{Like a senior professor who's advised grad students for twenty years, chatting with a student in the office.}

{Problem selection judgment, method maturity, experimental integrity, writing quality.}

{Verdict: strong accept / weak accept / borderline / weak reject / strong reject, one-sentence reason.}

* Inspiration

{Try connecting from three angles — transfer / remix / inversion. If it hits, expand; if all miss, say "none".}
